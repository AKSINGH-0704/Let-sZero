There is no single free-email limit: the number depends on the provider’s current plan, what counts toward its quota, and the period in which sends are measured. Before choosing a service, verify the live allowance for your account and calculate whether it covers your actual recipients, test sends, and scheduled follow-ups. Comparison pages can help you build a shortlist, but treat their figures as leads to confirm—not as your account’s current sending limit.[1][2]

## First, define what “emails” means

A headline quota is useful only when you know its denominator. A provider might describe an allowance by day or month, or distinguish campaign messages from contacts, subscribers, or other account activity. Those are not interchangeable measures. Check the provider’s plan page and the limit shown in your account, then look for the terms that define the quota.

For planning, count the messages you intend to send, not just the size of your contact list. A campaign sent to 300 recipients is a different workload from a list of 300 contacts that will receive several separate sends. Include tests, follow-ups, and any other planned messages if the provider counts them toward the same allowance.

## Compare limits on the same basis

The table below is a decision aid, not a directory of provider quotas. It avoids fixed plan numbers because free allowances can change and the supplied comparison sources do not establish the limit currently available to your particular account. Record the provider’s own current terms before committing to a campaign.

| Check | What to record | Why it matters | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allowance | Exact number shown in the provider’s current plan or account | Gives you the quota to compare | High when confirmed in current provider terms or your account |
| Denominator | Per day, per month, per campaign, or another stated period | Prevents comparing unlike limits | High only when the terms define it clearly |
| What counts | Whether the allowance includes tests, follow-ups, or other sends | Helps calculate the full campaign workload | Confirm in the provider’s terms; do not assume |
| Account conditions | Any eligibility, verification, or feature conditions attached to the allowance | A listed plan may not tell you whether your account can use it | Confirm for your account |
| Source date | Date you checked the terms | Makes later re-checks easier | High for tracking your own research; it does not guarantee terms will stay unchanged |

Use a provider’s own current plan information as your decision source. Treat editorial roundups, including the supplied bulk-email comparisons, as discovery aids that may need re-checking.[1][2]

## A practical go/no-go procedure

1. **Estimate the workload.** Write down recipients per send, number of sends, tests, and scheduled follow-ups. If you do not know whether a message type counts, mark it as unconfirmed rather than silently excluding it.
2. **Find the current account-specific allowance.** Check the provider’s current plan terms and your account. Record the date, period, denominator, and any conditions.
3. **Compare like with like.** Put your total planned messages beside the allowance for the same period. If you cannot establish the period or what counts, the comparison is not ready for a go decision.
4. **Leave room for uncertainty.** If your plan depends on the maximum listed allowance or ambiguous counting rules, reduce the campaign, split the work only if the provider’s terms allow it, or choose a suitable plan. Do not treat a planned increase as available until confirmed.
5. **Re-check before sending.** Confirm the account’s current limit and campaign setup immediately before a large send. If the estimate exceeds the confirmed allowance—or the terms remain unclear—pause and resolve the gap first.

## Keep free quotas separate from sending caps

A provider’s free-plan allowance and a sending cap or warm-up policy answer different questions. A cap can limit how many messages a sender sends within a period even when a campaign contains more recipients. RepMail by LetsZero has a default warm-up sending-cap ladder described in its product information; that is not a statement of a free-plan quota. The ladder and duration are configurable, so confirm current settings rather than using it to infer how many emails you can send for free.

Evaluate quota, sending policy, and other features as separate line items.

## Before-you-send checklist

- [ ] I recorded the provider’s current allowance and the date I checked it.
- [ ] I know the quota period and what the provider counts as a send.
- [ ] My estimate includes recipients, tests, and planned follow-ups.
- [ ] I checked conditions that apply to my account, not just a comparison-page summary.
- [ ] My planned workload fits the confirmed allowance for the relevant period.
- [ ] If a limit or counting rule is unclear, I have paused rather than assuming.
