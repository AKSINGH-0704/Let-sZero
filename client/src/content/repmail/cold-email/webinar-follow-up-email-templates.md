Webinar follow-up works best when it starts with what happened, delivers the promised value, and offers a relevant next step. Do not send one generic message to everyone: an attendee, a registrant who missed the event, and a speaker have different contexts.

## Segment before you write

For attendees, reference the session or question they engaged with only when that information is reliable. Give them the replay, slides, or a useful takeaway before offering a conversation. For registrants who missed the event, lead with the replay and acknowledge that they may not have been able to attend. For speakers or contributors, focus on the agreed publication and handoff rather than treating them like prospects.

Attendance is not proof of buying intent. Registration is not automatically permission for an unrelated sales sequence. Keep the follow-up aligned with the notice, consent, opt-out, and suppression process that governs the program. The [unsubscribe requirements checklist](/repmail/learn/compliance/cold-email-unsubscribe-requirements) is a useful operational reference; it is not a substitute for reviewing your own obligations.

## A simple event path

Send the promised replay or resource first. Then, if the recipient’s context supports it, share one additional item that helps them apply the topic. Close with one optional CTA: ask whether a specific problem is active, offer a short exchange, or invite a reply with a question. Avoid stacking a replay link, a calendar link, a product tour, and several downloads in one message.

Use the templates below as starting points, not claims about performance. Replace placeholders with the actual session topic and a resource you can stand behind. If the replay is gated, state that clearly. If the event covered multiple use cases, send the version that matches the recipient’s stated interest instead of guessing.

Before activation, compare the message to your [cold-email templates](/repmail/learn/cold-email/cold-email-templates), use a [low-friction CTA](/repmail/learn/cold-email/cold-email-cta-examples), and review the [complete cold-email guide](/repmail/learn/cold-email/complete-guide-to-cold-email).

## Edge cases to handle

If the replay is delayed, tell registrants when it will be available rather than sending a broken or placeholder link. If a registrant attended only part of the session, use the same attendee path without implying they saw every section. If the webinar included a partner, confirm which party owns the follow-up and which audience can receive it. If the recipient asks for no further messages, record and honor that request immediately. These small state distinctions keep an event workflow useful without turning every registration into a sales lead.





## Route the webinar state, not the registration record
The audience here is an event program’s registrants, attendees, and contributors. The trigger is the recorded webinar state—attended, registered-but-missed, partial attendance, asked a question, or contributed as a speaker—not the existence of an email address in an event export. Evidence should come from the event platform or a verified owner note, with the event name, session date, and promised resource recorded before copy is selected.

| Recipient state | Evidence and trigger | First message job | Owner | Measure | Stop rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attended | Attendance or reliable engagement record | Deliver recap or replay and connect one takeaway to the stated topic | Event marketer | Resource clicks, relevant replies, opt-outs | Stop after promised value and one optional next step |
| Registered, missed | Registration plus no attendance record | Acknowledge they may have been unavailable and provide replay or summary | Event marketer | Replay use and replies, separate from attendees | Stop if replay is unavailable or preference is unclear |
| Partial attendance or question | Timestamp, question, or moderator note | Answer the question or point to the exact segment; do not imply full attendance | Content owner | Questions answered and follow-up replies | Stop when the question is resolved |
| Speaker or contributor | Contributor agreement and publication status | Confirm published asset, attribution, and agreed handoff | Partner or speaker owner | Corrections completed and partner response | Stop when the agreed handoff is complete |

Select one template after the state is verified. An attendee can receive a concise recap, an absent registrant needs the promised replay first, and a speaker needs a publication confirmation rather than a prospecting CTA. If the platform reports attendance but the record lacks a usable permission or notice context, route it for program review instead of silently adding the contact to a broad sequence. Registration is evidence of an event action, not evidence of current buying intent.

A usable template has five slots: **event context**, **promised value**, **specific relevance**, **one optional action**, and **easy correction or decline**. Example scaffold for an attendee: “You joined [session] on [date]. The [replay or recap] covers [specific topic], and [resource] expands the section on [problem]. Is [one focused question] still relevant, or should I close the loop?” For a missed registrant, remove any language that implies attendance. For a speaker, replace the sales question with “Please confirm the published link and attribution are accurate.” These are message structures, not performance claims.

The event marketer owns segmentation and the promised asset. A content owner verifies the recap and links. A partner owner handles contributor corrections. The sender checks the current suppression state immediately before activation; if a partner supplied the audience, the program owner records which party is responsible for the next message. Keep the replay URL and its availability timestamp so a delayed asset does not produce a broken or misleading touch.

Measure cohorts separately: delivered, replay or resource use, replies, qualified conversations, questions resolved, opt-outs, complaints, and messages skipped for missing evidence. Compare attendees with no-shows only within the same event and message purpose. A high click rate cannot establish permission or intent, and a low reply rate may simply mean the promised resource answered the need.

Stop when the promise is fulfilled, the question is answered, the contributor handoff is complete, the event context has expired, the replay is unavailable, or the recipient asks for no further messages. A later campaign needs a new audience rationale and state; it should not inherit the webinar record by default.
