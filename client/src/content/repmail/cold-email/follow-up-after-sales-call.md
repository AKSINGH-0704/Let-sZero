A strong follow-up email after a sales call is a shared record, not a second pitch. Send a concise recap that confirms what was agreed, labels what is still uncertain, assigns owners, and proposes one dated next step.

## Build the recap from evidence

Start with the recipient’s stated problem or priority, using their language where possible. Then separate the record into three parts: confirmed decisions, open questions, and actions. Do not turn a polite possibility into a commitment. If the buyer said they would “look into it,” write that as an open item rather than an assigned task.

Use an owner and date for every action. “RepMail will send the security overview by Friday” is actionable; “we will follow up soon” is not. If the date is provisional, say so. Include promised documents or links in the same message so the recipient does not need to search another thread.

## A practical structure

The template in the asset below is intentionally plain. Put the most useful material before the call to action. End with one next step: confirm the summary, answer the open question, or schedule the agreed conversation. A recipient should be able to reply “yes,” correct one line, or say the timing changed.

For complex calls, ask an internal colleague to check the recap against the notes before sending. Keep internal speculation out of the customer-facing message. If multiple stakeholders attended, name the owner for each action rather than assuming the most senior person owns everything.

## What to do after sending

Record the recap, promised materials, owner, and next date in the opportunity record. If the recipient corrects the summary, update the record and use the correction as the new source of truth. If there is no response, follow your documented cadence rather than sending the same recap repeatedly; the existing [follow-up timing guide](/repmail/learn/cold-email/how-many-follow-ups) can help you choose a deliberate next touch.

Keep the ask low friction. A [clear CTA](/repmail/learn/cold-email/cold-email-cta-examples) might ask which of two questions is most important, rather than demanding a full meeting. Before the message enters a sequence, run the [sequence quality checklist](/repmail/learn/cold-email/cold-email-sequence-quality-checklist). For broader context, see the [complete guide to cold email](/repmail/learn/cold-email/complete-guide-to-cold-email).

## Edge cases to record

If the call ended without a decision, do not force one into the recap. Record the question that remains open and the person who can answer it. If a promised document contains sensitive or changing information, send the approved version and name its date or revision. If stakeholders disagree, summarize the disagreement neutrally and ask which point should be resolved first. A useful recap is accurate even when the outcome is “not yet.”
