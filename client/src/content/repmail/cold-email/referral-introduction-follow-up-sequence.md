A referral introduction follow-up sequence should acknowledge the introducer, explain why the connection is relevant, and give the referred person an easy way to accept, redirect, or decline. A warm introduction changes the context; it does not guarantee interest or permission for an open-ended sequence.

## Start with a clean handoff

Reply promptly while the introduction is current. Thank the introducer and address the referred person directly. Use only the context actually included in the introduction or otherwise verified. Do not write “you and I both know” or imply a personal relationship that does not exist. If the introducer has not made the intended owner clear, ask who should handle the topic.

The first reply should be short: identify the relevant problem, ask one focused question, and propose a small next step. If the recipient is not the right person, make it easy for them to route you or say so. Keep the introducer on the thread when that is useful and expected; move to a direct thread only when the handoff is clear.

## Use a small, state-based sequence

A useful second touch adds one relevant resource or observation. It should not repeat the original pitch or reveal private information from the introducer. A final close-the-loop message gives the recipient control: you can say that you will stop following up and leave a route to restart if the issue becomes timely.

Document the source of the referral, the date, the stated reason for the introduction, and any opt-out or suppression signal. The [cold-email compliance recordkeeping guide](/repmail/learn/compliance/cold-email-compliance-recordkeeping) can help structure those records; it does not make a legal determination for your situation. Use a [focused CTA](/repmail/learn/cold-email/cold-email-cta-examples), and adapt the [existing template library](/repmail/learn/cold-email/cold-email-templates) rather than implying that every referral needs the same cadence.

The sequence templates below keep attribution and consent expectations visible. For foundational context, see the [complete guide to cold email](/repmail/learn/cold-email/complete-guide-to-cold-email).

## Edge cases to handle

If the introducer gave only a name and no business context, ask for the missing context rather than inventing a reason to contact the person. If the referred person says someone else owns the problem, thank them and route the conversation cleanly. If the introducer asks not to be copied, respect that boundary and do not use the thread as social proof. If the referral conflicts with an existing opportunity or suppression record, pause and reconcile the records before sending.

## Protect the relationship

A referral is also a relationship-management task. Keep the introducer’s trust by being precise about what you are asking and by making the close respectful. If the recipient is not interested, accept the answer without asking the introducer to apply pressure. The quality of the handoff matters more than extending the sequence.
