As of October 25, 2026, the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 are in force in stages: Rules 1–2 and 17–21 commenced on publication, Rule 4 is scheduled for one year after publication, and Rules 3, 5–16, and 22–23 are scheduled for 18 months after publication. The notification was published on November 13, 2025, making the later dates November 13, 2026, and May 13, 2027. This is general information, not legal advice.

## DPDP Rules 2025 commencement dates

The Rules do not share a single commencement date. The notification assigns different start points to groups of provisions. Use the official notification and Rules text as the basis for your tracker, and confirm the source documents if you rely on this timeline for a formal compliance decision.

| Rule group | Commencement trigger | Calendar date | Status on October 25, 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rules 1–2 and 17–21 | Publication of the Rules | November 13, 2025 | In force |
| Rule 4 | One year after publication | November 13, 2026 | Not yet in force |
| Rules 3, 5–16, and 22–23 | Eighteen months after publication | May 13, 2027 | Not yet in force |

The dates above follow the publication date and staged commencement set out in the official notification. The official Rules text identifies the numbered provisions. [1] [2]

## How to use the timeline

Treat the dates as milestones for readiness planning, not as a substitute for reading the relevant provision. A provision’s commencement date tells you when it takes effect; it does not, by itself, resolve how it applies to a particular organization, activity, or data flow.

A practical process:

1. **Record the source date.** Save the notification date, November 13, 2025, and the official document versions used to calculate the later milestones.
2. **Break your tracker into rule groups.** Keep Rules 1–2 and 17–21 separate from Rule 4 and the 18-month group. Avoid a single “DPDP effective date” field.
3. **Assign an owner and evidence for each relevant provision.** Record who is assessing applicability, what process or system may be affected, and what evidence the owner will review.
4. **Set review points before each date.** Schedule an internal review before November 13, 2026, and another before May 13, 2027. Recheck official materials for changes or clarifications before relying on the dates.
5. **Document applicability separately from readiness.** Mark a provision “not yet commenced” separately from “not applicable” or “implementation complete.” These labels mean different things.

### Worked example

Suppose an operations team is maintaining a rule-readiness register on October 25, 2026. It can mark the first group as already commenced, flag Rule 4 for review ahead of November 13, 2026, and place the 18-month group in the May 13, 2027 planning cycle. If a team has not yet determined whether a provision applies to its activities, the tracker should say “applicability review pending,” rather than treating the future commencement date as proof that no action is needed.

## Keep commencement separate from cross-border analysis

The commencement schedule answers *when* provisions take effect. It does not, on its own, settle every question about transferring or accessing personal data across borders. Keep that assessment distinct from timeline tracking; see [adequacy versus safeguards in cross-border routing](/repmail/learn/compliance/adequacy-vs-safeguard-cross-border-routing).

For organizations managing outreach through agencies or multiple clients, document who owns the review and how relevant workflows are checked. The [agency cross-border review workflow](/repmail/learn/outreach/agency-cross-border-review-workflow) and [cross-client suppression architecture](/repmail/learn/outreach/agency-cross-client-suppression-architecture) cover adjacent operational questions. These resources do not replace a provision-by-provision legal assessment.
