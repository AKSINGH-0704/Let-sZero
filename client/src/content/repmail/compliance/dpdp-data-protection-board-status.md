The November 2025 notifications did not make every obligation under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act operational on the same day. They commenced specified provisions, including the provisions establishing the Data Protection Board, while setting later commencement dates for other provisions and rules. For compliance owners, the key distinction is between the regulator’s institutional setup and the later start of substantive duties.

## What the November 2025 notifications did

Read the notifications together, but do not treat them as interchangeable. One notification sets commencement dates for provisions of the DPDP Act. The separate DPDP Rules notification also specifies when particular rules take effect. The Act itself was enacted in 2023; the later commencement notification determines when specified provisions begin operating.

The Act’s commencement notification brought the Board-related provisions into force from the notification date in November 2025. That established the statutory basis for the Board’s constitution and functions. It did not, by itself, mean that every data fiduciary obligation under the Act had already commenced.

The Rules notification uses a phased schedule too. Some rules commenced on publication, while other rules were assigned later dates. The notification’s schedule,not a general assumption that all rules became effective together,is the source to use when planning a particular duty.

## Separate the dates before you plan

For each requirement, make a short record of four things: the legal instrument, the provision or rule, its stated commencement date, and the action your organization needs to take. Keep the Act’s commencement schedule distinct from the Rules’ schedule.

| Question | What to check | Practical result |
|---|---|---|
| Is this about the Board? | The Act provision establishing or governing the Board, and its commencement entry | Record the institutional commencement separately from business duties |
| Is this a duty under the Act? | The exact Act section and its listed commencement date | Do not assume it started when the Board provisions did |
| Is this a rule-specific requirement? | The rule number and the Rules notification’s commencement schedule | Apply the date stated for that rule |
| Is the date unclear or the provision cross-referenced? | The original notification text and relevant provision | Escalate for legal review instead of inferring a date |

### A compact decision procedure

1. Identify the exact requirement,not just the Act title.
2. Find its section or rule in the official text.
3. Check the matching commencement entry in the relevant notification.
4. Record whether it was effective in November 2025 or is scheduled for a later date.
5. Assign preparation work separately from the date a legal duty commences.

For example, if a team is reviewing a process because it involves personal data, first identify the particular obligation the process may engage. Then check that provision’s commencement entry. The Board’s establishment is not a substitute for that provision-by-provision check.

## What this means for compliance operations

Use the November 2025 date as an institutional milestone, not as a blanket effective date for all DPDP requirements. A phased schedule still matters even when an organization chooses to prepare early. Label internal controls as either preparation, an already-commenced requirement, or a requirement with a later commencement date. That makes it easier to avoid both premature legal conclusions and last-minute operational work.

If your review includes vendors or cross-border handling, keep those workstreams distinct from the commencement question.
