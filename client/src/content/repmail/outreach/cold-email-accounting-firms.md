# Cold Email for Accounting Firms: A Service-Trigger Workflow

Cold email for an accounting firm works best when it starts with a public business trigger, not an assumption about the firm’s finances. Use a hiring page, new service page, office expansion, technology announcement, or published planning cycle as the research starting point. A trigger only earns a relevant question; it does not prove that the firm has a problem.

## Build the trigger record

Capture the source URL, publication date, exact observation, likely role, and a confidence note. For example, “the firm published a page about a new advisory service” is observable. “The firm needs help with tax compliance” is an unsupported inference. Keep tax and legal advice out of the outreach workflow.

Write one service hypothesis and one low-friction question. A safe structure is: “I saw [public fact]. We help firms evaluate [business process]. Is [role] the right person to ask whether this is relevant, or should I close the loop?” Add a correction path: “If I have the wrong context, reply and I will update the record.”

## Suppression and timing

Before import, screen the address against the firm’s do-not-contact list and your own suppression record. The FTC requires a clear opt-out for commercial messages, and the ICO says corporate-body outreach is treated differently from messages to individuals, sole traders, and some partnerships [1] [2]. Do not infer permission from an address alone. Keep the unsubscribe workflow separate from seasonal planning.

Review the source again before a follow-up. If the trigger has changed, remove the old claim instead of pretending it is current. Measure replies using the [reply-rate measurement workflow](/repmail/learn/outreach/cold-email-reply-rate-measurement), but do not turn a result from one firm into a general benchmark. RepMail is useful for keeping sender events and suppression state observable; it does not validate a tax-related claim for you.

## Qualify the accounting lane first

Separate audit, tax, outsourced finance, advisory, and implementation. Use public service pages or announcements, not inferred client finances. Record source URL/date, service lane, likely role, and the question the source supports. A logo or “serves growth companies” claim proves neither capacity nor authority.

| Field | Evidence | Stop/route rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service | page or announcement and date | Stop when lanes are mixed |
| Role | practice leader or routing contact | Route to general inbox if unsure |
| Trigger | hiring, launch, deadline, published issue | Stop on confidential client inference |
| Fit | size, geography, system, workflow question | Stop when regulated data is requested |
| Proof | dated case study/capability page | Qualify as illustrative |
| Reply | positive, referral, no, opt-out | Stop after clear opt-out |

Review claims about savings, compliance, or records with a second person. Consult the [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) and [ICO direct-marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/). Send a small cohort only; do not expand when wrong-person, bounce, complaint, or unsubscribe signals rise.
