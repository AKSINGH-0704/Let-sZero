# Cold Email Software Pricing Models: Per Seat, Per Mailbox, and Usage

The right way to compare cold email software pricing is to normalize the billable unit first. A seat, a connected mailbox, a contact or send allowance, and a flat workspace fee measure different things. Compare the same operating scenario across all of them, then add the infrastructure and data costs the headline plan may leave outside the product.

Commercial terms are not universal: pricing, the definition of a billable seat, currency, billing term, renewal date or mode, and any autopay behavior are workspace- and configuration-dependent unless the current server-authoritative commercial state says otherwise. Use the current quote or checkout state as the source of truth; do not infer an automatic renewal or a next charge from a plan label or this worksheet. RepMail's current seat catalog is an INR/India catalog, not a global USD price list, so do not convert it into one.

## A practical way to evaluate billing units

1. **Write the operating scenario: users, sending mailboxes, domains, contacts, monthly sends, and required integrations.**
2. **Record each vendor’s billing unit, included limits, overage rule, contract term, explicitly documented renewal or payment behavior, and add-ons from a dated public page or quote.**
3. **Convert every option to cost per month for the scenario. Keep uncertain or quote-only items as ranges rather than guesses.**
4. **Run a sensitivity check for a second scenario, such as one additional rep or an idle month.**

## Decision table

| Model | What the bill tracks | What to normalize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per seat | Named users or roles | Users, permissions, and shared work |
| Per mailbox | Connected sending identities | Mailboxes, domains, and rotation policy |
| Flat-rate or tiered | Workspace or capacity tier | Included limits and upgrade thresholds |
| Usage or credits | Contacts, sends, or data actions | Expected consumption and overage |

## Edge cases and limits

A cheap plan can become expensive when a team adds mailboxes, verification, data credits, or a second workspace. Do not turn a vendor’s marketing label into a deliverability conclusion; price and sending outcomes are separate questions.

## A normalization example
Suppose a team needs three users, six sending mailboxes, two domains, and a monthly volume that may vary. A per-seat quote should not be compared directly with a per-mailbox quote until both are expressed for those quantities. Record whether a mailbox is an included capacity, a connected external identity, or an add-on. Do the same for contacts and sends: a contact allowance may count imports, active prospects, or enrichment actions differently. Keep one row for mandatory extras and another for optional conveniences. If the team expects seasonal demand, calculate a high month and a low month rather than multiplying the current month by twelve. That makes the decision traceable when procurement revisits the purchase.
