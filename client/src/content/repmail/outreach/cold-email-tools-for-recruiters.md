# Cold Email Tools for Recruiters: Compliance and Workflow Fit

Cold email tools for recruiters should be judged on candidate-data controls and human workflow, not only sequence features. Confirm who can view candidate records, how opt-outs and do-not-contact decisions propagate, how replies reach the responsible recruiter, and how records are exported or deleted. Vendor defaults do not establish legal suitability.

## A practical way to evaluate recruiting workflow

1. **Separate candidate sourcing, enrichment, outreach, and applicant-system records in the process map.**
2. **Define role access, approval gates, retention, suppression, and reply ownership before a pilot.**
3. **Test an opt-out, duplicate candidate, reassignment, and offboarding scenario.**
4. **Review current requirements for every recruiting market and document the owner of each decision.**

## Decision table

| Workflow | Test case | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate privacy | view and export sample | Fields and access are bounded |
| Suppression | candidate opts out | All relevant sends stop |
| Ownership | recruiter changes | Reply and history follow policy |
| Retention | candidate is closed | Deletion or archive is documented |

## Candidate-data scenarios to test
A useful pilot includes a candidate who asks not to be contacted, a candidate whose email changes, a duplicate profile from a second source, and a recruiter who leaves the team. Check whether the suppression decision follows the person across lists and sequences, whether the old address remains searchable, and whether the replacement owner receives the conversation context. Record which fields are copied into the outreach tool and which remain in the applicant system. Keep candidate records out of a production pilot unless the organization has approved the data flow. A clean operational handoff is more valuable than a long feature list because it shows who can act when a candidate exercises a preference or asks about their data.
