Create a single, concise sales-to-delivery handoff record that captures commitments, exclusions, deadlines, and success criteria so account teams don’t lose promises at kickoff. Use the record as a binding reference at kickoff and for change control: if something isn’t in this document, treat it as out-of-scope until renegotiated.

## What this handoff record is (and isn’t)

This document is a one-page operational artifact that translates sales promises into delivery obligations. It is not a full intake form, campaign brief, or kickoff project plan — those come after and should reference the handoff record. The decision boundary: include any commitment made to the client that affects scope, timeline, acceptance criteria, or exclusions; exclude routine delivery details that belong in the onboarding checklist or task tracker.

Evidence limits: this template captures commitments the account rep made and the client acknowledged; it does not supersede signed contracts or statements of work. If a contract differs, surface the discrepancy immediately to legal or the sales lead for resolution.

## Key fields and who owns them

List minimal, discrete fields to avoid ambiguity: Client name; Sales owner; Delivery owner; Signed SOW reference; Start date; First deliverable date; Promised outputs; Exclusions; Milestones and acceptance criteria; Renewal/benchmark dates; Escalation contacts. Each field should have an owner (sales or delivery) and a timestamp for last update.

Ownership rules: sales owns accuracy of what was promised; delivery owns feasibility and estimated effort. If delivery cannot meet a sales promise, delivery must raise a change request within 3 business days and note the impact on timeline, margin, or outcomes.

## Practical sequence to create and use the record

1) At deal close, sales fills the handoff record with promised outputs, exclusions, dates, and references to the SOW. 2) Immediately after close, send the draft to the delivery lead with a 48-hour review window. 3) Delivery updates feasibility notes, confirms acceptance criteria, and signs off or raises a change request. 4) The signed handoff accompanies the kickoff agenda and becomes the baseline for any change-control requests.

Stop conditions: if delivery doesn’t sign off within the review window, escalate to the sales manager and pause non-critical client-facing commitments until resolution. If an item remains disputed, log it in the record as “disputed — pending change request” and avoid delivering that item until formally agreed.

## Common failure modes and how to prevent them

Failure mode: implicit promises in email threads get lost. Prevention: require that any client-visible commitment added after handoff be appended to the record and acknowledged by both owners. Failure mode: delivery assumptions differ from sales assumptions. Prevention: include concrete acceptance criteria (e.g., “10k targeted opens in 30 days” as an example of measurable acceptance) and have delivery confirm feasibility or provide an alternative within the 48-hour review.

Decision boundary for bulk changes: minor scheduling tweaks can be handled in the onboarding plan; any change that affects deliverables, price, or deadline must trigger a documented change request and re-signed handoff entry.

## Using the record in change control and disputes

Treat the handoff record as the first line of evidence in any scope disagreement. When a client claims a lost promise, present the handoff record and the timestamped sign-offs. If the handoff lacks clarity, collect supporting artifacts (emails, SOW language, recorded calls) and open a change-control ticket with both owners’ notes.

Evidence limits: this record is persuasive internal evidence but may not replace contract language in legal disputes. If the disagreement could trigger financial or legal exposure, involve legal and billing teams before honoring the disputed commitment.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Fill the handoff record at deal close and attach SOW reference and date.
- [ ] Assign a delivery owner and set a 48-hour delivery review window.
- [ ] Include explicit acceptance criteria for each promised output.
- [ ] Document exclusions and items explicitly not included.
- [ ] Log disputed items as “pending change request” with timestamps.
- [ ] Require dual sign-off (sales + delivery) before kickoff agenda distribution.
- [ ] Treat post-handoff client commitments as change requests until re-signed.
- [ ] Escalate unsigned handoffs after 48 hours to sales/delivery leadership.
- [ ] Archive the signed handoff with kickoff notes and the onboarding tracker.

## Where RepMail fits

Use this handoff record as a checklist within outbound workflows: attach it to the kickoff cadence and require sign-off before sequences launch. It functions as a decision aid for whether a new client request is a minor scheduling change or a scope change requiring change-control. Do not assume RepMail or any outbound tool automatically enforces sign-off—use the record as the human-approved baseline.
