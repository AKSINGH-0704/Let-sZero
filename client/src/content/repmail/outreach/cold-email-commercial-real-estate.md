# Cold Email for Commercial Real Estate: Property-Trigger Outreach

Commercial real-estate outreach should be anchored to a verifiable property or business trigger: a public listing, announced development, tenant change, opening, or published market update. The trigger is a reason to ask a careful question, not permission to infer a private transaction, valuation, financing need, or investment outcome.

## Build a property record

Capture the property or portfolio identifier, public source URL, publication date, exact fact, likely role, and correction path. Separate “the listing says availability is being marketed” from “the owner is motivated to sell.” The latter needs evidence you may not have. Avoid promises about returns or market performance.

A useful opening names the source and asks for routing: “I saw [public property fact] in [source]. We work with teams evaluating [specific service]. Is [role] the right person for a brief fit check, or should I close the loop?” Keep tenant and contact data to the minimum necessary.

## Handle outreach responsibly

Screen against suppression before every sequence and provide a clear opt-out. CAN-SPAM sets requirements for commercial email, while ICO guidance distinguishes corporate bodies from sole traders and individuals [1] [2]. Do not treat a corporate domain as proof that every person at the organization should be contacted. If the property fact changes, correct or retire the draft rather than sending stale personalization.

Track replies by trigger type and role using the [reply-rate measurement guide](/repmail/learn/outreach/cold-email-reply-rate-measurement). If a recipient disputes the fact, record the correction and stop using it. RepMail can keep sending and suppression events visible, but it does not provide private property data or investment advice.

## Qualify property triggers without private inferences

Start with a public listing, development announcement, tenant change, opening, broker page, or market update. Capture URL/date, property identifier, geography, stakeholder organization, and the operational question supported. A listing does not prove ownership, financing need, vacancy, valuation, or authority.

| Field | Evidence | Stop/route rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property | public URL, identifier, date | Stop if stale or ambiguous |
| Stakeholder | role and organization | Route when authority is unverified |
| Trigger | development, opening, tenant, update | Stop on private-finance inference |
| Offer | specific operational help | Qualify projections and case studies |
| Response | reply/referral/no/opt-out | Stop after opt-out or complaint |
| Audit | approval, cohort, events, owner | Pause incomplete evidence |

Consult the [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) and current [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126). Run a small cohort and compare qualified replies, not opens alone. Do not expand when wrong-person or unsubscribe signals rise; keep confidential deal information out of campaign notes.
