# Cold Email for Agencies Entering a New Vertical: Proof Translation

When an agency enters a new vertical, translate proof by problem and process, not by swapping an industry name into a case study. A result from one client is not automatically typical, and a logo is not permission to imply an endorsement. Keep the evidence, scope, date, and limitations attached to every claim.

## Build a proof map

Record the original client, permission status, problem, work performed, measured outcome, time window, and what cannot be generalized. Then map the new vertical’s comparable workflow. If the workflows are not comparable, use capability language rather than outcome language: “we have a process for reviewing…” rather than “we will produce…”

Separate verified facts, estimates, and hypotheses. Do not promise typical results. FTC endorsement guidance says claims must be honest and not misleading, and material connections should be disclosed when they would affect evaluation [1]. Route testimonials, customer names, certifications, and competitor references through a reviewer.

Use the [agency tools guide](/repmail/learn/outreach/best-cold-email-tools-for-agencies) for operational comparison, then run the [vertical QA checklist](/repmail/learn/outreach/vertical-outreach-qa-checklist) before sending. Keep an easy opt-out and truthful sender identity; CAN-SPAM applies to commercial email [2]. RepMail can support event and suppression visibility, but it cannot make an unverified case study safe or typical.


## Operate a bounded launch

Turn the proof map into a launch record that another reviewer can audit. Give each claim a stable ID, the original source URL, the date checked, the permission status, the exact wording approved, and the person who owns the review. Add the comparable business problem and the limit of the comparison. “We have reviewed this workflow” is a capability statement; “you will achieve this outcome” is a result claim that needs evidence the agency may not have. Keep those two kinds of copy in separate fields so a later edit cannot silently broaden the claim.

Use three decision states. **Ready** means the evidence, permission, audience fit, sender identity, and suppression check are complete. **Review** means a source or approval is pending and a named owner has a due date. **Stop** means the source cannot be verified, the permission is unclear, the new vertical is not comparable, or the copy would imply a typical result. Do not move a record from review to ready because a deadline is close.

A small implementation example is a proof card for a new healthcare-services track: original problem, process delivered, date, measured result as reported, attribution limits, and approved sentence. The campaign owner then adds one new-vertical hypothesis and a neutral question. The QA reviewer checks the card, the agency owner approves the wording, and the SDR receives only the approved sentence. If a prospect asks whether the result is typical, the reply owner uses the limitation recorded on the card rather than improvising. Track sends, replies, qualified conversations, opt-outs, and complaints separately, and archive the card when its source or permission expires. See the [SDR-to-expert handoff workflow](/repmail/learn/outreach/vertical-outreach-sdr-handoff) when a prospect asks for evidence the sender cannot substantiate.


## Build a claim ledger before changing the audience
This article is for an agency owner and reviewer entering a vertical where its existing proof may not transfer. The trigger is a proposed new-vertical campaign or a request to reuse a client result—not a vague belief that the agency “works with similar companies.” Before copy is written, compare the original workflow with the target workflow and record what the evidence does and does not establish.

| Claim ledger field | Example of a safe entry | Owner | Send decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claim ID and source | P-014; dated client report or approved case study | Agency owner | Required before review |
| Original problem and work | “Reviewed routing rules for a five-person support team” | Delivery lead | Keep the scope intact |
| Observed outcome | “Client reported fewer manual checks during the measured period” | Delivery lead | Quote the period and attribution |
| Transfer boundary | “Target has a different system and regulatory workflow” | Vertical reviewer | Use capability language or stop |
| Approved sentence | One sentence with qualifier and no typicality claim | Copy reviewer | Only this wording enters the draft |
| Expiry and permission | Permission status, checked date, recheck date | Account owner | Archive when stale or withdrawn |

The evidence chain should answer four questions: what happened, who observed it, when it was measured, and why the target workflow is comparable enough to discuss. A logo, screenshot, or percentage without scope is not a transferable proof point. If the target vertical has a different buyer, process, data sensitivity, or success definition, describe the agency’s method and invite a diagnostic question rather than borrowing the result. “We have reviewed this class of workflow” is materially different from “you should expect the same result.”

Message logic should follow the evidence boundary. Lead with the target recipient’s likely operational problem only when there is a verifiable basis for the fit. Then use one approved proof sentence, its qualifier, and one neutral question about whether that problem is active. Do not add a customer name, certification, competitor comparison, or outcome adjective during personalization. If a prospect asks whether a result is typical, the sender should quote the ledger’s scope and limitation or route the question through the [SDR-to-expert handoff workflow](/repmail/learn/outreach/vertical-outreach-sdr-handoff).

Ownership is deliberately split. The agency owner decides whether to enter the vertical and accepts commercial risk. The delivery lead confirms what was actually done. The vertical reviewer checks comparability and audience language. The copy reviewer controls the approved sentence, while the SDR may select from approved variants but may not broaden them. Keep the reviewer and due date attached to the campaign record, not in a chat thread.

Measure proof translation rather than only campaign activity. Compare sends, positive replies, qualified conversations, opt-outs, complaints, and “not relevant” replies by claim ID and message version. Review whether a claim produces clarification requests or skepticism; those are evidence-quality signals, not merely copy problems. Do not present a small pilot as proof of typical performance, and do not combine different verticals until their workflows and evidence definitions are comparable.

Stop the launch if the source or permission cannot be verified, the outcome cannot be attributed, the target workflow is materially different, or the approved sentence would imply a guarantee or endorsement. Also stop an individual message when the recipient corrects the premise or asks not to be contacted. Re-entry requires a new proof record or a documented change in the target workflow.
