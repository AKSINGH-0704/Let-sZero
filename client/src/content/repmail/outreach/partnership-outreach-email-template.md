# Partnership Outreach Email: A Research-First Template

A useful partnership email is specific about the mutual opportunity and modest about what is proven. Before writing, identify the partnership lane, verify the business context, and decide who owns the next step. The worksheet below keeps a referral, integration, co-marketing, or event idea from becoming a generic sales pitch.

## Research before copy

Record the recipient's role, the public source for the relevant business context, and the audience overlap you can explain in one sentence. Then write a collaboration hypothesis: what would each party contribute, who would benefit, and what is the smallest test? “We should partner” is not a hypothesis. “A 30-minute technical fit review for the teams using X” is closer, but it still needs evidence.

Do not claim a customer, integration, event slot, or result until it is confirmed. If a referral fee, affiliate payment, free product, or other material connection exists, add a disclosure checkpoint. FTC guidance treats endorsements as claims that must be honest and not misleading, with material connections disclosed when they would affect evaluation [3].

## Choose the modular message

For a **referral** ask, name the audience overlap and ask whether a reciprocal introduction process is worth discussing. For an **integration**, cite the public documentation you checked and ask for a technical owner; do not claim compatibility from a logo or marketplace listing alone. For **co-marketing**, specify the proposed asset, audience, and owner. For an **event**, state whether the contact is an attendee, sponsor, speaker, or organizer and keep those contexts separate.

A compact skeleton is:

> Subject: A specific partnership idea for [audience]
>
> I noticed [verifiable context]. We work with [relevant audience]. There may be a fit around [concrete collaboration], but I have not assumed the integration or audience overlap is already confirmed. Would you be open to [small next step]? If this is not relevant, reply “no” and I will stop.

The sender still needs truthful identity and an appropriate opt-out path. CAN-SPAM applies to commercial messages, and the ICO distinguishes corporate recipients from individuals and sole traders [1] [2]. Measure partnership replies separately from cold prospecting; use the [reply-rate measurement guide](/repmail/learn/outreach/cold-email-reply-rate-measurement) without promising a response rate. RepMail can provide the sending and suppression workflow, while your team supplies the research and relationship judgment.
