# Cold Email Tool Contract Questions: Data, Exports, and Reputation

The most important cold email software contract questions concern exit and responsibility, not only features. Clarify who owns records and suppression decisions, what can be exported, when data is deleted, how renewal works, and which party operates domains, mailboxes, authentication, and incident response. This is a business review checklist, not legal advice.

## A practical way to evaluate contract review

1. **Mark the data, identity, and operational assets your team must retain.**
2. **Ask for current terms, order form, data-processing terms, subprocessor list, SLA or support terms, and deletion process.**
3. **Tie each verbal answer to a written clause or dated vendor response.**
4. **Have legal and security reviewers resolve jurisdictional and liability questions before signature.**

## Decision boundary: sign, condition, or walk away

Sign only when the order form, terms, data-processing terms, support terms, and security answers agree on ownership, export, deletion, renewal, incident duties, and sending identity. Choose **conditional approval** when a gap has a named clause, compensating control, owner, and deadline. Walk away or hold when the vendor will not state what is exportable, who controls suppressions, what happens to backups, how renewal notice works, or which party bears an operational duty. “Included” and “unlimited” are not acceptance criteria without scope and measurement.

## Contract-question worksheet and worked check

Record `question_id`, `document_name`, `version_or_as_of_date`, `exact_clause`, `vendor_contact`, `answer`, `owner`, `risk`, `fallback`, `approval`, and `review_date`. Ask separately: Who owns raw contacts and event history? Can suppression records be exported with reason and effective time? What is deleted at termination, including backups? Who owns domains, mailboxes, DNS, authentication, and provider complaints? Which support channel handles an incident and what is actually committed? How are price, seat, credit, usage, renewal, and notice terms defined? A practical completeness calculation is `answered critical questions / total critical questions`; if 8 of 10 are answered, the 80% figure is context only—conditional approval still fails if either missing answer concerns suppression or exit.

## Failure cases and stop condition

Common traps include a marketing page promising export while the contract limits it to CSV, a “free trial” that uses different retention or roles, an auto-renewal clause with a different notice period than the quote, and a reputation guarantee that is actually a best-efforts statement. Stop signature if the document versions conflict, legal or security review is incomplete, or the vendor asks for production data before safeguards are approved. Preserve the rejected version and redline; do not rely on a verbal correction.
